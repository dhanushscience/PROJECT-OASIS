OASIS Privacy Policy

Version 2 · Last updated 24 November 2026

In short

  • OASIS turns your speech into text on your own PC. Your voice never leaves it.
  • No account. No ads. I never sell your information or use it to train models.
  • One optional thing can leave your PC: three numbers a day. It is off until you say yes, and OASIS works exactly the same if you say no.
  • This page follows India's Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023 (the "DPDP Act") and its 2025 Rules, which I follow now even though their main duties begin in 2027.

Who I am

I am Dhanush, the developer of OASIS, and I decide why and how this information is used (the DPDP Act calls this a "Data Fiduciary"). You can reach me, and the person who handles privacy questions, at oasis-privacy@protonmail.com.

What is never collected

What you say or type, any dictated text or audio, names, email addresses, phone numbers, file names, window titles, program names, websites, your location, a device or advertising ID, or your IP address. OASIS does not give your computer any lasting ID (each summary gets its own random number), so I cannot follow one person from one day to the next.

Why, and only why

I use the numbers for one thing: to understand whether OASIS is useful and improving, and to show that progress to people who help fund the project. I do not use them for anything else, and I do not sell or share them.

Stored on your PC

Settings, profiles, button setups and any dictation history stay on your PC, so you manage and delete them in the app. Dictation history is opt-in. A Groq key, if you add one, is encrypted by Windows. None of this reaches me.

Other connections OASIS can make

None of these carry the three numbers, and none involve me receiving anything from you.

  • Updates and speech models. OASIS checks GitHub for a newer version and downloads speech models from there. GitHub sees a normal download request, like any website visit, under its own policy.
  • Optional cloud cleanup (Groq). Only if you add your own Groq key. Then the text of a dictation goes from your PC straight to Groq to be tidied, under Groq's policy and not mine. Leave it out and your dictation never leaves your PC.

Your choice (consent)

  • OASIS asks once, after the first-run guide, and asks again if this policy changes. The question explains the same things as this page, in short, before you can answer.
  • Saying yes means ticking a box that says you are 18 or older, then pressing Yes. Nothing is pre-ticked.
  • Under 18? Please leave this off. I do not knowingly collect anything from children, and the numbers contain nothing that would let me tell your age.
  • To change your mind: Settings, Privacy, Turn off. This is as easy as turning it on.
  • When you turn it off, OASIS stops counting, deletes the counts waiting on your PC, and sends nothing more. A summary that was already sent earlier cannot be taken back (see "Your rights" below).

Who receives the numbers

  • Aptabase (aptabase.com), an analytics service, using its EU region. It acts only on my behalf (a "Data Processor") under its data processing agreement. By its own privacy policy, it uses your network address only in memory to receive the message and does not store it.
  • No one else. I do not sell, rent or share the numbers.

How long they are kept

  • On your PC: a day's counts, and a summary waiting to be sent, are kept until they are sent, and are dropped after 14 days if they cannot be sent. They are deleted when you turn this off.
  • At Aptabase: by its policy, for up to 5 years. After that they are gone. I will also delete the whole set if I stop using the service.

Your rights under the DPDP Act

You can ask me to: tell you what I hold about you; correct it; erase it; or fix a problem with how I handle it. You can also name another person to exercise these rights for you if you die or cannot.

One honest limit: the numbers are not linked to you (no name, no ID, no address), so I cannot find "your" numbers among everyone else's, and cannot show or delete them one by one. What I can always do is stop collecting (you can do this yourself by turning it off) and tell you exactly what OASIS sends.

To ask anything, or to complain, write to oasis-privacy@protonmail.com. I will answer as quickly as I can and always within 90 days. If I have not solved the problem, you may complain to the Data Protection Board of India.

Keeping it safe

The numbers travel over an encrypted connection (HTTPS). On your PC, the counts sit in a small file that holds only the three numbers and dates, and summaries waiting to be sent sit in another that holds exactly what is listed under "What is collected". The OASIS code is public, so anyone can check what it sends.

If something went wrong that put anyone's data at risk, I would tell the people affected and the Data Protection Board without delay, with a full report within 72 hours as the Rules require. Since nothing identifies you, a notice would be posted with the OASIS release notes on GitHub.

Changes

If I change what is collected or why, I will change the version number and the date at the top. OASIS then stops sending until you answer the new question, so a change can never apply to you without your yes.
